How it works

From headline to outcome range

Markets rarely meet a truly new event. Brahmanda finds the moments that looked most like today and measures what happened next.

Read the event

A language model turns the headline into a meaning vector, so "Fed signals cut" and "rate relief ahead" are recognised as the same kind of news.

Find precedents

It searches the event history for the closest matches, weighting similar wording, similar market conditions and recency.

Measure what followed

Price moves after those precedents are combined into a probability distribution at 7, 15 and 30 days: the median, the 5–95% range and the shape of the tails.

Flag the risk

When precedents disagree, split into two camps or lean heavily to one side, the event is flagged, because that is where surprises come from.

Illustrative example

"Gold jumps as investors seek safety after missile strikes"

  • Closest precedents: earlier safe-haven rallies after military escalation
  • Outcome range: median move and 5–95% band at 7, 15 and 30 days
  • Shape: whether outcomes split into two camps (fade or follow-through)
  • Red flag: raised when the downside tail is heavier than usual

Brahmanda comes out of PhD research on event-driven forecasting in oil and gold markets, developed and backtested on historical market data. The terminal puts that method in front of anyone with a headline.
